We’ll formalize the single-population model with dynamic permeability. This is the version that produced the most interesting results: bistability, hysteresis, noise-induced tipping, and crisis-induced permanent closure. The equations below are exactly those used in the simulations, cleaned up and with all variables and parameters defined.

---

## 1. State variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Range |
|--------|---------|-------|
| \(U\) | Actual unresolved environmental uncertainty (0 = fully known, 1 = fully unknown) | \([0,1]\) |
| \(B\) | Boundary strength / closure (0 = none, 1 = total closure) | \([0,1]\) |
| \(T\) | Trust capacity (0 = none, 1 = full) | \([0,1]\) |
| \(E\) | Exploratory capacity (0 = none, 1 = maximal) | \([0,1]\) |
| \(P\) | Boundary permeability (0 = fully opaque, 1 = fully open to information flow) | \([0,1]\) |

All variables are dimensionless and bounded.

---

## 2. Auxiliary quantities

Define the **felt uncertainty** \(F\) as:

\[
F = \frac{s\, U}{(1+\lambda T)(1+\mu B)}
\]

Here \(s\) is the **stakes / uncertainty multiplier**: higher \(s\) means the same actual uncertainty \(U\) feels more threatening. Trust \(T\) reduces felt uncertainty linearly via \(1+\lambda T\); boundary strength \(B\) does the same via \(1+\mu B\). This captures the idea that both trust and boundaries are ways to make the world feel more predictable, even if they do not reduce actual uncertainty.

Define the **effective boundary suppression block** \(Q\) as:

\[
Q = (1-P)B
\]

This is the part of the boundary that actually suppresses exploration and trust. If permeability \(P\) is high (near 1), even a strong boundary \(B\) does not block information flow or destroy trust. If \(P\) is low, the boundary is opaque and damaging.

---

## 3. Differential equations

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot U &= n(1-U) - \alpha E (1-\beta Q) U \\
\dot B &= \rho_B \, \sigma\!\big(k_B (F-\theta)\big) - d_B B \\
\dot T &= \rho_T E (1-\beta_T Q) - d_T T - \gamma Q T \\
\dot E &= \rho_E \, \sigma\!\Big( k_E \big( \frac{\alpha U}{1+\eta Q} - c_E \big) \Big) - d_E E \\
\dot P &= \rho_P \left( 1 - \sigma\!\big( k_P (F-\theta_P) \big) - P \right)
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})\) is the logistic sigmoid. All parameters are positive constants. The term \(n(1-U)\) represents the natural inflow of new uncertainty from the environment (bounded by 1). The second term in \(\dot U\) is the reduction of uncertainty through effective exploration; boundaries reduce exploration's effectiveness through \(Q\). The dynamics for \(B,T,E\) have standard first-order decay with rates \(d_B,d_T,d_E\), and growth terms driven by sigmoid activation functions.

## 5. Governance interpretation

Each term has an institutional meaning:

- \(\dot U\): uncertainty grows from the environment and is reduced by exploratory activity. Boundaries reduce exploration's ability to resolve uncertainty, especially if opaque.
- \(\dot B\): boundaries are built when felt uncertainty exceeds a tolerance threshold \(\theta\). They decay slowly if not reinforced.
- \(\dot T\): trust grows through successful exploration under open conditions. Opaque boundaries (\(Q\) high) erode trust.
- \(\dot E\): exploration is triggered by high actual uncertainty relative to a cost threshold \(c_E\). Opaque boundaries reduce the drive to explore.
- \(\dot P\): permeability is not fixed; it drifts downward when felt uncertainty exceeds \(\theta_P\). Thus, crises can degrade boundary quality endogenously.

This model combines the boundary–trust tradeoff with dynamic boundary quality, giving rise to the rich behaviors we observed.

---

## 6. Notes on model scope

- The model is deterministic; noise can be added to \(F\) to study stochastic tipping.
- All variables are clipped to \([0,1]\) in numerical integration.
- The dynamics are intended as a **minimal** representation, not a calibrated empirical model. It captures qualitative mechanisms, not quantitative predictions.
- The two-population version couples multiple copies of this system through shared \(U\), but each population retains its own \(B,T,E,P\).
